Where Snowdaes is today

What the website does, what Clover does, and the things I think are true — written down so you can tell me where I'm wrong.

The website

snowdaes.com is a lovely-looking page. It tells people who you are, shows the six things you make, and says Billerica is open. What it doesn't do is sell anything.

There's an Order Online button. It doesn't take you to a menu — it takes you to a page asking which shop you want. Then that sends you somewhere else again.

Clover is doing the real work

Everything that actually matters runs through Clover: the menu, the prices, the toppings, the card payments, the receipts, the reports. It's your till, your kitchen ticket and your bookkeeper at once. That part works, and nothing I'm proposing changes it.

In plain terms

Think of Clover as the kitchen and the till. Think of the website as the front door and the menu board. Right now you have a very nice front door that doesn't open — so everyone has to walk round the side to a different door that Clover built for you.

That side door is snowdaes-north-billerica.cloveronline.com. It works. It's just not yours: it's a Clover template with your name at the top, the web address isn't your name, and you can't change how it looks or what it says.

The gap, in one sentence

You can take an order online today. What you can't do is give someone a reason to come back.

What Clover gives you

  • Orders, on the same screen as walk-ins
  • Card payments and one bank deposit
  • Inventory and prices in one place
  • Receipts and reports

What nobody gives you

  • Discount codes — not limited ones. None.
  • A list of your own customers
  • A "quiet Tuesday" offer you can send out
  • A website that looks like Snowdaes
Worth saying plainly

Ordering already works. If all you want is ordering, you don't need me — and I'd say so. The reason to do anything here is the second column.
