Either they stopped serving it, or a whole category is missing from the listing. Worth asking him, because it changes what the menu is.

Two corrections to my earlier notes. I proposed a wide grotesque; their sign is condensed, so all three mocks follow the sign. And the green is brighter than the #1F6B45 I guessed — closer to a kelly green. Every green here is still estimated from a photograph and must be replaced with the real value from his artwork before anything ships.

One more, minor: the door reads 1552 while the ordering page says 1652 Center Point Pkwy. Probably me misreading the sign at that resolution — worth a glance next time you are there.

A · the storefront, translated

The sign becomes the interface. Kelly green band, white condensed caps, the Hibachi · Asian · Philly Steak bar kept verbatim. Black hairline frames echo the storefront glazing.

Loud, warm, unmistakably this building. Somebody who has driven past it recognises the site instantly.

Risk: heavy caps and hard borders can read as shouty on a long menu, and green fields fight orange food photography.

B · quiet, photography-led

Chrome gets out of the way. Near-white ground, food does the colour, green appears only on the reorder button and the selected tab.

Built for the ninety-second repeat order. It is also the direction that survives mediocre photography best, because nothing else is competing.

Risk: the safest, and the least like them. Without the sign’s voice it could be any restaurant with a green button.

C · the hybrid is the structure

Takes the tagline literally and makes it navigation. Three doors, three stripe colours pulled from the building — sign green, wok orange, roof rust.

Dark ground so the stripes and the food carry all the colour. The only direction that treats the Philly steaks as a real half of the business rather than an oddity.

Risk: the hibachi door may be empty — see above. And a dark restaurant menu is a real bet: it photographs well and reads worse in a sunlit car.

What I would pick

A, with B’s restraint below the header. The green band and the tagline bar buy instant recognition at the top of the screen, and then the menu itself calms down into tiles so the food and the prices do the work.

C is the most interesting idea and I would not build it yet. It hangs on hibachi being a real part of the business, and right now the only evidence for that is a sign. If he confirms hibachi is live and material, C becomes the strongest of the three — it is the only one that describes the restaurant he actually runs.

B alone is the safe answer. It would work. It would also be the version he could buy from anyone.

Before any of this is real

  • The green. Every value here is estimated from a photo. One file from him replaces all of it.
  • The photographs. Blocked out on purpose. Who owns those photographs is still unanswered, and B depends on the answer more than A or C.
  • Hibachi. On the sign, absent from the menu. Decides whether C exists.
  • The badge. Rendered as plain “AK” type here. The real roundel is his artwork and should be used as-is rather than redrawn.
