Where this actually stands, what I'd want from each of you, and what I'm hoping to get out of it — because I should be straight about that part too.

Where it stands right now

Everything I've shown you runs on my laptop. Nobody else can see it. If I close the lid, it's gone until I open it again.

In plain terms

It's a cake I've baked in my own kitchen. It's real, you could eat it — but it's in my kitchen, not in your shop window. Putting it somewhere the public can reach is a separate job, and it costs a bit of money every month. I haven't done it yet, because I don't want to build the wrong thing first.

The payments I showed you were run against a practice copy of Clover — a pretend shop with your menu in it, that Clover gives away free to people building things. No real money has moved and I've never touched your actual account.

In plain terms

Like a training till. Same buttons, same receipts, fake money. It means I can make mistakes without any of them landing on your books.

What I'd need — Option A

If you want the simpler one, the site that looks good and hands over to Clover:

  • Permission to use your photos and the penguin. I've been using them as placeholders. I need a yes, or I need to take them off.
  • Real reviews. The site currently shows three made-up ones I wrote to fill the space. They're invented, and they come off before anyone sees this publicly.
  • Who owns snowdaes.com and who can log in to it.
  • Confirm the opening hours and whether "ready in 15–20 minutes" is right.

That's genuinely most of it. Option A is a few weeks, evenings and weekends.

What I'd need — Option B

Everything above, plus two things only you can give me:

  1. Two access codes from your Clover, per shop

    You make them yourself in your Clover settings — about five minutes each, once two-factor login is switched on. Nobody from Clover is involved, nothing gets installed, and you can cancel them any time. Think of it as cutting me a key to one specific door, that you can take back.

  2. Twenty minutes at the shop with the Station

    I want to watch what happens when an online order comes in today, and check whether a ticket prints. That's the one thing I genuinely can't test from home.

One question that changes the build

How often do you refund an order? Daily, weekly, or almost never? It sounds like a small thing, but it decides which of two ways I take the payment — and it's much cheaper to know now than to find out in December.

Questions I'd rather ask in person

Brian — you're at the counter. When an online order comes in, what actually happens? Does the Station beep? Does someone have to be watching it? What goes wrong on a busy Saturday — missed orders, wrong toppings, people turning up too early?

Jennifer — is any of this a priority? If online ordering is 5% of your day and the real problem is staffing or the Lowell lease, tell me and I'll stop. I'd rather be useful than busy.

Both — would you actually use a discount code? Everything I've argued rests on that being worth something to you. If you wouldn't run one, Option A is the honest answer and it's a lot less work.

What I want out of it

I should say this plainly rather than let you wonder.

I'd like to come and work in the shop, maybe from November. Not as a favour — I want to learn how the business actually runs. What a Saturday rush feels like, where the money goes, what breaks, what the suppliers are like.

I'd like to open one in Alabama. So the more of this we sort out before November — the website, the ordering, what maintaining it looks like month to month, whether campaigns actually bring people back — the more useful I am to you while I'm there, and the more I understand before I try it myself.

That's the trade, and I think it's a fair one: you get a website and someone who cares about it, I get an education you can't get from the outside.
