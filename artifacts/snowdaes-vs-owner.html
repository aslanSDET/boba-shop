The $499 version

Before I got too pleased with myself, I went looking to see whether I'd invented something or rebuilt something. Rebuilt. A company called Owner.com sells almost exactly this, to restaurants, for $499 a month. Here's what they do, what it costs, and the handful of things they're better at that I'd happily copy.

Who they are

Owner.com builds ordering websites for independent restaurants — the same pitch as Option B in the last document: stop paying DoorDash a third of every order, and get your customers ordering from you directly. They've been at it for years, they're funded, and they have a real support team.

What a shop gets for the money:

  • A website with ordering built in
  • A phone app with the shop's own name and logo on it
  • Automatic emails and texts to past customers
  • A rewards programme — a punch card that lives on the phone
  • Suggested add-ons at checkout
  • Delivery drivers, arranged for you, around $7 a run

And they plug into Clover. Their own words: "Owner has a native integration with Clover, and connects to Toast, Square, Lightspeed and more." Orders land on the Clover screen next to the walk-ins, no extra tablet.

In plain terms

It's the difference between hiring a caterer and cooking yourself. The caterer turns up with everything, does it well, and charges every month for as long as you use them. They're not doing anything magic — they're doing the work you'd otherwise do, and they're good at it.

They recommend the $499 plan once a shop is doing more than $5,000 a month online. There are no contracts and no cancellation fee — you can stop any month. Pricing is quoted per restaurant, with "special rates available for multi-location," which they don't publish, so two shops is somewhere between $499 and $998 a month until someone asks them.

There's one more fee, and it's the one worth noticing, because it isn't paid by Snowdaes — it's added to the customer's bill. They call it a 5% order support fee.

One thing I couldn't find out

Nowhere on their site or in their contract does it say who pays the card-swipe fee on an online order — the 2–3% the bank always takes. Their agreement says they send you the money "less the Transaction Fees," which reads like it comes out of your side, but they never spell it out. If you ever talk to them, that's the question to ask.

In plain terms

Owner.com is a friend collecting money at the door of your party and handing you the cash at the end of the night, minus what they're owed. It works. But for a few days, a company you've never met is holding Snowdaes' money, and every month you get two sets of numbers to line up.

Mine is the customer paying your own till. One deposit. Nothing to reconcile. When Brian opens Clover on Monday, the online orders and the counter orders are already sitting in the same list.

To be fair to them: refunds and chargebacks land on the restaurant either way — their contract says so plainly, and so does Clover's.

Does the money work out?

So they're a clear win over DoorDash once the orders are there, and an expensive way to find out whether they will be. Their own customer reviews say the same thing in plainer words — the most common complaint is that the monthly cost feels steep before the results show up. One shop's testimonial on the other side: "we've made more than $50,000 in sales and saved ourselves $10,000 in commission."

Worth knowing before you'd ever sign anything

The other complaint that comes up repeatedly in their reviews is trouble with the Clover connection specifically — more than with the other tills they support. I can believe it. I've spent the last few weeks in Clover's plumbing and it is genuinely awkward in places. A funded company with a dedicated team is fighting the same thing I am.

What I'd steal from them

Here's the useful part. Almost everything they charge for is a good idea, and most of it is a good idea we could do too. The one they've clearly thought hardest about is the thing I haven't built at all: going back to people who already bought once.

Every direct order builds your customer list. More data, more connections, and more chances to bring them back.

Owner.com's own pitch

That's the whole game, and it's the part DoorDash quietly takes from you: when someone orders Snowdaes on DoorDash, DoorDash gets the phone number, not Snowdaes. Order directly and the phone number is yours. Which means you can do this:

"We haven't seen you in a while"

A text to anyone who ordered once and then went quiet for a month. This is the single highest-returning thing any shop does, and it costs a few cents a message.

The second order

The first order is the hard one to win and the second is the one that makes a regular. A small code sent two weeks after someone's first ever order is aimed exactly there.

Hot Tuesday

Ninety degrees in Billerica and the shop is quiet at 3pm? Send twenty percent off for the next two hours. Clover's own ordering page can't run a code at all — ours already can.

School's out, Lowell

A standing Friday 2:30 message to the Lowell list only. The two shops have different crowds and there's no reason they get the same message.

Birthday drink

One free drink on the day, asked for at signup. Costs about $2 and it is very hard to collect it without buying something for whoever came along.

The tenth one free

The punch card you already understand, except it can't be lost in a wallet, and it counts across both shops.

In plain terms

All six need the same one thing first: a place to remember customers. Right now the site is a very good waiter with no memory — it takes the order perfectly and forgets you the moment you walk out.

Giving it a memory is a filing cabinet: names, phone numbers, what they ordered. It's a known job, it's not exotic, and I deliberately haven't done it yet, because it's the first piece that costs a little money every month and I didn't want to spend yours before you'd seen anything.

Two smaller ones I'd take from them straight away, no filing cabinet needed:

  • Add-on suggestions at the till. "Add boba for 75¢?" right before paying. The site already knows every topping in your Clover, so this is a small piece of work rather than a new system.
  • Best sellers at the top. The site has a POPULAR badge sitting there unused, because Clover doesn't mark anything popular — we'd work it out from what actually sells. Better than my guess, and better than alphabetical.

One claim of theirs I'd treat carefully: they say their sites get "2 to 4 times more orders than the average restaurant website." That's their marketing, measured by them, and I've no way to check it.

So what am I actually saying

Not "don't use them." If a year from now Snowdaes wants the texts and the rewards and the drivers all running on their own, with somebody paid to answer the phone when it breaks, $499 a month is not a crazy price for that and I'd tell you to take it.

What I'm offering is a different trade. No monthly bill, nothing added to your customers' tab, and the money going straight into the Clover account you already have. In exchange, the marketing side doesn't exist yet, and the support line is a family member in Alabama.

I'd rather you heard that from me than found it on Google afterwards.
