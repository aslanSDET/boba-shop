The markup

The pattern is not uniform, and that is the interesting part. Mains, drinks and the one premium side carry 17–50%. Plain sides and plain wings carry nothing at all. Someone marked up the items that sell, and left the cheap attachments alone.

This is the strongest argument the project has, but it needs one caveat said out loud: a markup is not the commission. It shows the shop already prices around the platform. What the platform actually takes from them is on their statements, and still has not been established.

Missing entirely

Things on the board that have no representation in our build at all.

  • Hibachi — four dishes whole category

    Chicken $12.99 · Steak $16.99 · Seafood $16.99 · Combination $18.99, all marked “Coming Soon!” on the board. This closes a question that has been open in the notes since the start: hibachi is on their sign and absent from the menu because they have not launched it yet. It is on the delivery listing nowhere.

  • Philly sizes — Small, Regular, Large structure

    Every cheesesteak is sold in three sizes; our build carries one price each, and it is the small. An Original Cheesesteak is $6.99 / $9.49 / $12.49 on the board and a flat $7.49 in our data. Same for all six.

  • Drink sizes — Regular and Large structure

    Original Lemonade $2.49 / $3.49. Fruit Lemonade $2.99 / $4.29. Fountain and tea $1.99 / $2.49. Our build has a single price per drink and no size choice.

  • Philly extras modifier group

    Bacon +$1 · Extra cheese +$1 · Extra meat +$2 · Jalapeños +$1 · Banana peppers +$1. Also “Upgrade to combo, from $3.49”, which the listing mentions in prose but never as a choice.

  • Ingredients for all six cheesesteaks copy

    The board spells each one out — “Steak, grilled onions, mushrooms, green peppers, cheese, lettuce, tomato, mayo & pickle.” Our item sheet shows these six as name and price only. Six of the 29 items flagged as having no detail are answered outright by one photograph.

  • Calories on everything data

    Every dish, every entrée choice, every side carries a calorie count, and the combos carry ranges. Worth having on its own terms, and worth asking whether their location is required to display it.

  • Spice level data

    Seven entrées carry one or two chilli marks: General Tso, Blackpepper Chicken, Mongolian Beef, Pepper Steak and Blackpepper Shrimp at one; Kung Pao and Spicy Beef and Jalapeño Shrimp at two. Nothing in our build expresses heat.

  • Wings: fries or veg sticks, and drink size modifier group

    Each wing combo includes a choice of regular fries or veg sticks, plus a 20 oz drink — 32 oz on the extra large. We model neither.

Wrong, not just missing

Places where our build asserts something the board contradicts. These matter more than the gaps, because they look finished.

  • Family Feast is modelled as the wrong meal wrong

    The board: “3 sides, 2 entrées, 4 egg rolls & cookies · Serves 3–4.” Our build models it as three entrées and one side, reusing the Pick Any Three structure. It is a different dish. The listing’s own description says the same thing as the board and we did not carry it across.

  • Five entrées cannot be chosen in a combo wrong

    The board offers 15 entrée choices. Our combo group offers 12, and they are not a subset — Kung Pao Chicken, Mushroom Chicken, Spicy Beef, Pepper Steak and Jalapeño Shrimp are missing, while Spicy Grill Chicken and Chicken and Broccoli are offered and appear nowhere on the board.

  • Beef is overcharged in every combo wrong

    The board is explicit: premium charge +$1.00 beef, +$1.50 shrimp. Our build applies a flat +$1.50 to both, so every beef combo prices fifty cents high.

  • Wing flavours are a different list wrong

    Our build has six. The current board has seven, the listing’s own text names nine, and the three lists do not agree with one another. See below.

  • “No description” was our error, not theirs wrong

    The last pass concluded 49 of 68 items had no description anywhere. That was wrong — the listing carries descriptions our transcription simply did not capture: “Chinese fried rice with eggs, peas, and carrots”, “Original Lo Mein cooked with brown sauce, cabbage, and carrots”, “Crispy fries topped with creamy ranch dressing and a sprinkle of Cajun seasoning”, “Perfect combination of fried rice, shrimp, beef, and chicken”, and more. The owner does not need to write these; we need to re-read the source.

The boards disagree with each other

Two of the photographs show the wings panel, and they are different boards. The composite matches what the delivery listing was built from; the photograph of the wall is current. The menu changed and the listing was never updated.

What this changes

In the order it is worth doing.

  • Ask which price list is real before rebuilding anything

    Two boards disagree on wings, and the in-store and online prices disagree everywhere. Only the owner can say which is current and whether the online markup is deliberate. Rebuilding the data first risks encoding a third wrong answer.

  • Re-read the listing for descriptions we dropped

    Cheap, no owner needed, and it removes most of the “29 items with no detail” problem on its own.

  • Fix the four outright errors

    Family Feast composition, the five absent entrée choices, the beef premium, and the wing flavour list. These are wrong today regardless of which price list wins.

  • Add sizes as a real modifier group

    Philly small/regular/large and drink regular/large are the same shape as the entrée and side groups already in the schema. Doing this properly also sets up Square, where variations are how sizes are modelled anyway.

  • Decide whether direct ordering prices at the counter or at the listing

    The whole pitch is that the shop keeps the margin the platform takes. If the direct site charges in-store prices, the customer sees roughly 20% off and the shop still nets more. That is a decision for the owner, and it is the single most valuable question in this document.
