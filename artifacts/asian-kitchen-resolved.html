Combo builder

The screen that matters. Their Storefront asks for four separate “Select 1” dropdowns with the side wedged between the first and second entrée. This offers the three real popular orders first, then collapses the choice: because half of all orders are the same dish three times, Same for all three turns four decisions into two.

The “one tap” rows are the pitch. Not the colours — a regular ordering three times a week goes from roughly six interactions to one. That is the argument to make to him, and it is the reason “Your usual” needs somewhere to store an order (PLATFORM.md §4), which is the first thing on this project that costs money.

Decisions inside these screens

  • The side moved to the end. DoorDash puts it between the 1st and 2nd entrée. Nobody thinks about a meal that way.
  • Twelve entrées, six shown. The rest sit behind the list; the six are the ones that appear in the popular combinations.
  • Price deltas on the chip. Beef and shrimp are +$1.50 and that has to be visible before tapping, not as a total that silently jumped.
  • Underline tabs, not pills. Six categories in a row of pills is a wall; underlines let the food be the loudest thing.
  • No “Most Ordered” duplicate section. Their menu lists the same items twice, once under Most Ordered and again in their real category. One place each.

Still open

  • The green is estimated from a photograph. His artwork replaces it everywhere.
  • The roundel is set as plain type here. His real badge should be dropped in as-is, not redrawn.
  • Photography is blocked out. This design leans on it more than the alternatives did.
  • Hibachi is on the sign and absent from the menu. It sits in the tagline bar here on his authority; if it is gone, the bar changes.
